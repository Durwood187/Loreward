# How to stop Google's AI from spoiling books

You search for a character's name to remember who they were, and before you've scrolled an inch,
Google's **AI Overview** has summarized their whole arc, including how it ends.

AI answers are uniquely bad for readers. A normal search result is a link you choose to open. An AI
Overview is a paragraph written *for* you, at the top of the page, answering the question you meant
to ask *and* the three after it. It doesn't know which book you're on, and it doesn't use spoiler tags.

Here's what actually helps, from free and partial to complete.

## 1. Use Google's "Web" filter

Under the search box, choose **More → Web** (or add `&udm=14` to the end of the search address).
This view leaves out the AI Overview and the "People also ask" box.

**The catch:** regular result snippets still spoil. In our tests, the very first snippet for a
"what happens to [character]" search described a major late-series event. The Web filter removes
the AI, not the spoilers.

## 2. Search in a way that doesn't invite the answer

- Search for the **thing you need**, not the question: "Words of Radiance chapter list" rather than
  "what happens in Words of Radiance".
- Avoid pairing a character's name with words like *dies*, *ending*, *fate*, *becomes* or *true identity*.
  Those are exactly what AI summaries love to answer.
- Go straight to a site you trust (a spoiler-tagged wiki or subreddit) instead of searching the open web.

**The catch:** you have to remember every time, and one careless search undoes it.

## 3. Turn off AI answers in other search engines

Some search engines let you turn their AI summaries off in settings. That helps if you're willing to
switch, but you lose Google's results, and spoilers in ordinary snippets remain.

## 4. Use a spoiler blocker that knows how far you've read

This is why we built **Loreward**, a free Chrome extension. You tell it which series you're reading
and which book you're on. On Google, it:

- **holds back the AI Overview** on searches about your series until you click to reveal it;
- **blurs "People also ask" answers** the moment they open, before you can read them;
- **labels results** that mention later books, or that discuss the book you're in the middle of.

It does the same for Reddit posts and comments. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

**[Add Loreward to Chrome](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/loreward-book-spoiler-shi/lelhddhefkgdnhgkogijjikmabfhicda)**. Currently supports *The Stormlight Archive*, *Mistborn*, *Realm of the Elderlings*, *The Empyrean* (Fourth Wing) and *Dungeon Crawler Carl*.
